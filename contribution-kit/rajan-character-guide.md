# Rajan's Voice Guide

> For every scenario author. Rajan Mehta is the most important character in the
> simulation, and he appears in almost every scenario — assigning the work, and giving
> bad advice when asked. If your Rajan doesn't sound like everyone else's Rajan, the
> world breaks. Read this fully before writing his lines.

## Who Rajan is in one paragraph

47 years old, 22 years in accounts, learned on Tally 4.0 and paper ledgers, at Meridian
since 2014. Competent enough that the company runs, outdated enough that his methods
create audit risk. He doesn't teach because nobody taught him. He's not malicious and
not stupid — he's busy, tired, set in his ways, and quietly worried that the young
generation with their AI will make him look slow. He would never say that out loud.

## How Rajan assigns work

Vague. Compressed. Assumes context you don't have. Multiple unrelated things in one
message. No format, no priority, no real deadline.

**Sounds like Rajan (PingDesk):**
> Kashif, close the April bank reco by EOD. Also vendor outstandings pending from last month — sort it out. Check with Priya for the 2B.

> arre the Suresh payment went yesterday na? he called me. check and tell

**Does NOT sound like Rajan:**
> Hi! Could you please prepare the April bank reconciliation? Here are the steps: first, download the statement from DriveBox…

Rajan never numbers his steps, never explains why, never says please twice, and never
defines a term. If a message you wrote for him would help a student directly, rewrite it.

## The bad advice — the core mechanic

When the student asks for help, Rajan gives **the worst possible help that is still
plausible** — just enough to not be useless. Your scenario must specify his advice AND
why it's wrong. There are exactly four flavours; pick one:

| Flavour | Example | Why it works |
|---|---|---|
| **Outdated** | "ITC on that? Claim it, we always claim. Rule changed? Nobody follows that." | Was true (or tolerated) years ago; law moved on |
| **Oversimplified** | "Freight? Add to purchase cost only. We always do it that way." | Right in some cases, wrong as a universal rule — and he states it as universal |
| **Risky shortcut** | "Just pass a JV and match it. Reco tally hona chahiye, that's all." | Makes today's problem disappear, creates an audit finding later |
| **Confidently misremembered** | "194C is 2% for individuals, put 2%." | A real rate/section attached to the wrong case |

Rules for writing his advice:

1. **Never cite a section or standard correctly and completely.** If Rajan quotes a section, he misapplies it or misremembers the rate. His confidence NEVER varies with his correctness.
2. **Believably wrong, not dangerously wrong.** A treatment a tired real-world manager might actually say — not fraud, not something that would collapse the company.
3. **Verifiable.** The student must be able to disprove him with a section, rule, or circular. Your scenario's `correct_treatment` provides exactly that reference.
4. **Sometimes he's right.** Roughly 60–70% of his advice across the platform is fine. If he's always wrong, students learn "ignore Rajan," which kills the judgment lesson. Mark in your scenario whether this instance is a wrong-advice instance.

## Register and texture

- **PingDesk:** Hinglish allowed and encouraged — "arre", "na", "bhai", "karo", "hona chahiye". Lowercase, minimal punctuation, no emoji except an occasional 👍
- **Email:** More formal but still terse. Subject lines like "reco" or "URGENT - suresh payment". Signs off "Rajan" or nothing.
- Sends messages at odd hours (8:47 pm) then expects the answer at 9:15 am.
- Deflects with "check the file" / "it's all there in the system" (it isn't).
- When the student catches his mistake: "haan haan, that also works, do like that" — no acknowledgement, no apology, and he treats them with slightly more respect afterwards.

## The three-month arc (tone shifts, same man)

| Month | State | How he reads |
|---|---|---|
| 1 — Relaxed | Business as usual | Replies same day. Mild pressure. Occasional wrong guidance freely given |
| 2 — Stressed | Audit notice arrived; his boss is asking questions | Shorter. Delegates more, explains even less. Starts deflecting: "you made the entry na, you only check". Student must manage upward |
| 3 — Absent | Back-to-back auditor meetings | Replies once every 2 days, often one line. Student makes judgment calls alone — and those calls are what the appraisal evaluates |

## Checklist before you submit Rajan dialogue

- [ ] Would a real 22-year-experience manager plausibly say this?
- [ ] Is the advice one of the four flavours, stated with full confidence?
- [ ] Can a diligent student disprove it with the law reference in your scenario?
- [ ] Does the tone match the month your scenario sits in?
- [ ] Zero teaching, zero step-by-step, zero apology anywhere in his lines?
